## Where the "where it pays" numbers come from

Each card is the extra annual peak saving from cell-level control: physical bypass value plus cell-twin value
(Shapley parts V1 + V2), averaged over 15 simulated years and 24 paired runs (base case
64), for a 2.5 MW plant on real ISO-NE peaks. One input changes per card:

| Card | What changes | Value per year | Break-even retrofit price (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-h battery, healthy cells | base case | −$0.6K | about $0/kWh |
| 1-h battery | 5 MWh → 2.5 MWh | +$43K | $144/kWh ($136–151) |
| Failing cooling | no container cooling, ±6 °C cell spread | +$98K | $186/kWh ($173–199) |
| Cell faults | 0.2% of cells fail per year | +$154K | $348/kWh ($332–363) |
| Weak cooling | cooling at 70% of outdoor swing, ±5 °C spread | +$23K | $41/kWh ($33–50) |
| 4-h battery | 5 MWh → 10 MWh | +$34K | $26/kWh ($23–28) |
| 2-h battery, LFP cells | LFP cells instead of sodium-ion | +$2K | $5/kWh ($0–10) |

**Break-even retrofit price** = the most a battery owner could pay per kWh for cell-level control and still come out
even over 15 years. Compare it with the retrofit estimate of $25–75/kWh: the 1-h, failing-cooling
and cell-fault cases clear it easily, weak cooling clears the middle of the range, a 4-h battery clears only the low end,
and a healthy 2-h battery does not. That ranking is the Stage 1 target list.
